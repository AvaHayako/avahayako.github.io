<h1>Residents of Hansell Tree</h1>

<p>
  Leading up to this project, I'd experimented with stop-motion animation as a 
  
  This project was an experiment with combining one medium, watercolors, with another, 2d animation.  It's not a unique concept at all--in fact, it's only a hop and a skip away from an early form of animating called cel animation--but it was completely new to me. I'd only ever done a couple *short* stop-motions and messed around with the animation mode on Procreate. 
  
   Instead of handpainting cel sheets, I've hand-drawn every frame in ToonSquid. And instead of scanning those frames over top a watercolor painting, I've superimposed those frames over a digitized watercolor painting. I've seen a few folks call this digital cel animation!
  
  The result is short but sweet animation that is whimsical as heck and evokes a sense of nostalgia and peacefulness!

   Here's how I did it:
</p>

<h3> Motivation </h3>
<p>  </p>



<h2> Creating the background </h2>

<h3> Painting </h3>
<p>A few weeks before I came up with this project, I'd finished a watercolor painting that had been floating around in my mind for months. The concept of the painting was a floating rock in space with a great big tree at the very center. The residents of this floating rock are <italics>snailiens</italics>, who have made this tree their home by digging tunnels in and around it. After painting it with watercolors, gouache, and ink, I proceeded to digitize it. </p>

<h3> Digitizing <h3>
<p>After photographing the painting, I imported it into GIMP. I basically made it print-ready by straightening the edges, bringing out the texture, and tweaking the lighting and saturation. It was at this stage that I came up with the idea to bring this painting to life through 2d animation. To accomplish this I needed to remove the snails that were already there. I could have kept them and tried to animate them or around them, but I really wanted to push the boundaries of what I could do to evolve the painting. So the snails had to go. This is how the painting looked after this stage: </p>


<h2> Animating <h2>
<p>I could spend paragraphs detailing the animating process. In a nutshell, I used ToonSquid--a program I was completely new to--to layer snails over the painting and make them do their snail things. It took some time to figure out how to even animate, but I probably spend the most time figuring out the placement and the exact actions of the snails. It took even more time to figure out how the snails appearance changes as they move, turn around, and interact within the space.My clay snails came in handy here--I actually used them as a reference quite a few times in order to get the perspective right. I took time to enhance the overall scene: like adding a warm to the tree and the snails. For the stars and the pink cloud that were in the original painting, I added a subtle pulsating glow that you can only notice if you take the time to watch. Here's how the painting looked now:</p>

<h2> Editing and sharing on social media <h>
<p> So at this point, I had this wonderful scene with lots of moving parts. I wanted to share it online, but I had to figure out how to actually present it. I couldn't just post the 5 second video; on a tiny phone screen it'd be too difficult to view. I wanted whoever was viewing to be able to appreciate each snail and take in their movement, expression, personalities. At the same time, I loved the watercolor painting itself and I really wanted to emphasize that too. 

So I ended up making two videos. The first video zooms in on each individual snail, while the second uses wider shots to emphasize the surrounding environment. See for yourself!

<h3> Reflection </h3>
<p> Overall, including the painting, this project took about 4 weeks to complete... 4 weeks for a 5 second animation, gosh!!! 

I faced a lot of new project 

Honestly, it would have gone faster if I spent more time in the planning stage. Instead of storyboarding--which I have no idea how to do--all I did was say "yeah I definitely want these snails to do move" and dove right in. And instead of making rough sketches of the snails and specific angles, I just figured it out along the way. In total, I probably started from complete scratch ~at least~ 4 times.
